Collection editor: design notes

One definition per page. You edit on the left and see on the right what that definition matches right now, and why. Titles come from the real MDBList list; the counts (641 listed, 42 owned) are from the 2 Nov server spike.

  1. 1

    Header actions

    Save is the only primary button. "Delete definition" sits apart behind a rule and empty space, outlined in ink like any other button and turning red only on hover or focus, so it can't be hit while reaching for Save.

    Deleting a definition proposes deleting the box set through Review; it never deletes on the spot.

  2. 2

    Schedule as a timetable

    Instead of a date picker you can't check, the window shows its next three departures, saying exactly what will happen at each. "Relative rule" switches to nth weekday or Easter offset, plus lead and lag days.

    Hiding with a blocked tag was verified on your Jellyfin 12.4 (2 Nov), so it is offered beside Kometa's delete-and-recreate.

  3. 3

    Sources with health

    Each source shows its list size, owned matches and health on one line, with its full path and role on hover and in its options menu. A source can be set to add directly (the Kometa preset) or to suggest only (labelled "suggests"): wider IMDb keyword matches become Review proposals rather than members. This answers the brief's "suggestions" requirement.

  4. 4

    Per-collection modes

    The same Off / Review / Auto control as Settings. An underlined segment means "inherited", and an override is labelled. A plain line under the heading reminds you that dry run currently forces everything to Review.

  5. 5

    Live preview with provenance

    Tabs split what the box set would hold now from the diff (add, remove, excluded). Every row explains itself: the source and its rank, and how it was matched (IMDb ID against ProviderIds, or TMDb via /find). Pinned items say so.

    Pin and Exclude act inline, without a modal. On narrower screens the preview drops below the form, and a "Live preview · 42" button in the header jumps to it. Posters are typographic placeholders here; the app loads them from Jellyfin.
