Dashboard: design notes

Direction: Timetable. Built to answer your three questions in order: what needs me, what broke, what's next. Owned counts (Christmas 42, Thanksgiving 10, Veteran's Day 12) come from the 2 Nov server spike; review counts and service states are illustrative.

  1. 1

    Needs you

    Leads the page, as you asked. The count sits in a sentence, not a giant KPI tile, and the breakdown by collection links straight to a filtered Review queue.

  2. 2

    Service notices

    Failures are visible but do no harm. A red dot means failing, a yellow dot means served from cache, a hollow dot means OK. Every problem says what the app is doing about it, here "serving the last good result, removing nothing", and offers Retry.

    All the healthy services share one line, so the problems stand out. The last run sits here too, because it is part of "what broke".

  3. 3

    Next change, on yellow

    This is the only yellow panel: the timetable's next departure. It gives the exact local time (windows flip at midnight, America/New_York), what opens and what closes, and how big the plan is. "Preview the plan" shows the computed actions before they happen.

  4. 4

    Year strip

    All 19 Kometa windows packed into three lanes. Black means live, yellow opens next, an outline means scheduled. The red today hand nods to the station clock's red disc.

    New Year's wraps past 31 Dec, so it appears at both ends of the strip. Valentine's 02/29 is clamped to 28 Feb in non-leap years. Short windows such as Mother's Day and Disabilities show as slivers, with their name on hover and focus. On a phone the strip becomes a now-and-next list rather than shrinking to illegibility.

  5. 5

    Upcoming changes: the signature

    The schedule is set in a timetable's columns: Date, Change, Collection, Library, Via, Remarks. Month bands work like a timetable's hour groups. Changes due at the same minute are listed together, and the next departure is posted on yellow.

    Remarks carry exceptions: pending reviews, Kometa's 500-title cap, a failing source. On narrow screens whole columns drop in priority order (Library first, then Via and Remarks) rather than squeezing.

  6. 6

    Rail and dry run

    A black rail, with yellow marking where you are. Dry run is always shown in the rail footer, because it changes what every Approve and Auto means. Missing carries a Phase 2 tag rather than disappearing.

  7. 7

    Run now

    The one ink primary button on the page. Progress streams into the button and the Activity log through HTMX polling; there is no modal. While a run is in progress the button reads "Running… 2/5 sources".
